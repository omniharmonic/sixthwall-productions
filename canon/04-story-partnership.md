# Story Partnership

*The ethics of taking money. From the first third of the conversation, which
is a post-mortem on a commercial meeting that went wrong [T:7–69], and the
later return to what a partnership may and may not be [T:368–431]. No third
party is named here. The principles are what matter and they are general.*

---

## The name

"That's why it's story partnership, never brand partnership. We never say that
we're doing brand partnerships." [T:413]

## The virus

"I've been so antagonistic towards any kind of corporate for-profit structure
that I'm easing my way into a different orientation that recognises the
necessity — culturally and economically — to embed within those economic
structures almost like a virus that then consumes it from the inside out."
[T:9] "Not in a direct, clear way, at a plane of reality where we all are in
shared reality that's what's happening. It would be more like a metaphysical
kamikaze thing." [T:11]

"I can see more clearly pathways for a genuine gnostic intervention inside of
a pretty straight business partnership — because business partnerships are
the land of sellouts. You go there to sell out. That's the whole point. And so
to go there and to not sell out would require a particular set of conditions."
[T:19]

## The form that can carry it

"Sauna businesses, in particular, are unique sites of both culture and the
economy, in that they represent an ancient form of social bond. They're now
memetically hot in the biohacking and wellness community. And so there's a
consumptive element that's reaching for something that's also ancient. In
that liminal space of wanting to consume it, but it also carrying this innate
quality of the experience itself, it's a prime candidate for the religion
that's not a religion — because it has that consumptive quality that's getting
people in the same way that we led with the megachurch energy. Those are the
sticky bright lights that attract this root-based desire and longing that
humans have, and it's being met by people who are providing objects for them
to consume: the object of Christ, the object of wellness. It has just enough
genuine metaphysical gravity and enough hollowness in the artifice that we
could actually inject an anti-religion religion into that context. It's a
sufficient carrier function because it gets people in the door; they're open
enough; they think it's secular. What the anti-religion religion does is it
presents as secular, but then it brings people into a direct experience of the
sacred." [T:11–19]

"We wouldn't be creating a mystery school inside of Exxon Mobil." [T:19]

"It has to be a place where people congregate to become better. Saunas, or
gymnasiums — but not a yoga studio, because part of the business of a yoga
studio is that it takes itself really seriously, and yoga is itself a unified
path. You have your own mystery school there. It would disrupt the functioning
of effective things that are already spiritual practices. There's not space for
added rails or structure in those environments. But communal watering holes
that have an ethos of self-improvement on the individual, where like-minded
people come to meet each other — the form is perfect for that. People spend a
lot of time there. They gradually open. It has a psychoactive effect."
[T:38–41]

"There's a real alchemical fire burning in those places. The people who tend it
are actually really cool to talk to. If you can scale that to multiple cities,
into a place where people are looking forward to an unexpected conversation —
that very act of having an unexpected conversation with a stranger is what
life used to be: a tapestry of mutual performance, meeting new people in the
public square, in an unscripted way, in a place where the alchemical fire is
hot enough to help you process some of these stuck things and literally sweat
them out. The contrast of the hot and the cold tempers steel. If you can put
anywhere near a vulnerable and mutually open culture into that environment,
you can germinate something quite beautiful. Nordic spa, Roman bathhouse,
Greek bath, longhouse, sweat lodge, temescal — all linked to the forging of
community just as they are to the forging of steel." [T:542–550]

"It's a modular stretch. You can stay in the cold as long as you want. You can
dip into the mystery school as long as you want." [T:551–552]

## The tell

"That intensity of business language was unsettling." "And very archonic."
"Archonic, draconic, but unexpected to be sure." [T:36–38]

The specific phrases that set it off, kept here as a field guide: *we are
going to own the space*; *growing the pie for everyone*; *unlocking community
synergies*; *becoming a hub that everything goes through*. [T:30–32] And the
reply: "Even if that's true, why would you say it like that?" [T:31] — and, a
beat later, the shape that is wanted instead: "giving it all back — which is
actually a toroidal structure." [T:33–34]

"Give the people what they want." "No." "We're doing something much bigger
than that." [T:230–232]

"By being polite, by not immediately responding in kind, you tacitly agree to
alchemise some of that wayward energy until you come to a conclusion." [T:235]
A note to ourselves about meetings.

## The frame that negates itself

Benjamin's sincere version, from his own prior writing, quoted in the room:
"We live inside the stories we tell. I work with communities, coalitions and
visionary teams to surface and shape narratives that remember our
interconnectedness and regenerate shared meaning. Through co-creative media,
mythic framing, and cultural design, we cultivate cultures of wholeness where
values, voices, and visions can harmonise towards collective liberation."
[T:371]

And his own translation of it: "That's the sincere wholesome version of this,
where it's really: we'll help you build an ethical cult. We know that the
world is made of stories. Whatever you're doing would be more effective and
powerful if there was a meme-plex that animated it that actually works
psycho-spiritually and resonates with people's experience enough that they
start to live inside of the story world of whatever you're creating. That
creates a kind of collective coherence and momentum that you could extract
profit from — and then it wouldn't be an ethical cult." [T:373]

"The only cults that we're interested in building are ethical ones, which
illuminate their own structures. It can still be inside of a carrier wave — a
place like that would be cooler if there was a mystery school. But the mystery
school isn't *sign up your friends to ascend to the next level.*" [T:414]

"The ethics that we're ascribing to are a frame that negates itself, which can
direct genuine energy towards the thing that we're partnering with, but it
does so in a way that genuinely liberates people and supports them in being
the authors of their own stories." [T:428]

"It supports others in cultivating their verbs rather than merely establishing
the noun of the partnership. It encourages greater vibrational freedom in the
world rather than trapping that energy in established nouns." [T:429]

## Advertising already knows

"This is what advertising already does. We don't care what you're selling; we
will create a compelling set of feelings around it. We're going to create a
sigil for you, and then we're going to charge that sigil with a particular
emotion." "But we'll also give them mantras." "*Just do it.*" "*Have you heard
of God?*" "And gestures and sounds." "Whoa — you've been saying it backwards
your entire life." [T:387–395]

The difference is not technique. It is who holds the pen afterwards.

## The designed world and the main character

"One of the wild things about creating a fully encapsulated destination is
that you've created an entire world. And people are hungry to visit entirely
designed worlds. And then when you do it, you get to be the main character in
that world. It's getting people to, in a non-narcissistic way, design their
own world and reclaim their status as the main character within their world
without hiding, without flinching, and with integrity. If that world is a
benefit to others, that's a kind of heaven on earth — being able to wake up
every day as the main character in your own story and create beauty and
participate. Not just encapsulated by your own world; we don't want people to
just get their house in the hills and only live there." [T:384–386]

## What we learned from the meeting that went wrong

Kept without names, because the lesson is structural:

- The form was right and the content was not; those are separable judgements,
  and the right form is rare enough to be worth remembering on its own.
  [T:7–21]
- A partner on a different plane of reality is not a bad partner. It is
  information about how to relate to them. [T:25]
- Each pitch is an experiment, and each experiment produces people who want
  to use you, be used by you, love you, be loved by you, create you, be created
  by you. The form should allow the good ones. [T:55–59]
- Language that wants to own a category is a tell. Hear it early. [T:30–38]
- Do not wear the room's politeness as an obligation to alchemise its energy.
  [T:235]
