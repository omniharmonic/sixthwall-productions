# The Epoch

*What Sixth Wall Productions is doing, at the scale at which it is actually
doing it. The conversation names the scale once, in passing — "the contested
period of the consummation of the axial age" [T:183] — and then talks at that
scale for two hours without saying so again. This text says so. It should be
read before the others, and it is the corrective to a reading of them that
stops at method.*

---

## Three ages

**Before.** There were no spectators. A people experienced themselves as
participants in a cosmological process, acting out the archetypes that were
acting them, with no line between this world and the other one; ritual kept
the rhythm of that coherence [T:98]. Knowledge was plural, local and slow —
the psychotechnologies of regenerating a forest over a thousand years
[T:183]. The sacred was not somewhere else.

**The Axial split.** Across Eurasia in the first millennium before the common
era a new kind of god appeared: one, transcendent, outside the world, the same
everywhere. With him came the things the conversation is grateful for — law,
the word, the mind, "the chair you are sitting in" [Herd pamphlet] — and the
thing it is diagnosing: a split down the middle of experience. God above,
world below. Truth outside experience. A soul that watches a body. A people
that watches a priest. A house that watches a stage. The spectator position
itself is axial. So is the secular: a "this world" is only possible once there
is an "other" one.

The split won because it coordinates [T:183]. One transcendent story, consumed
by everyone, gets large numbers of people to act together — to build, and to
kill — in a way plural participatory traditions cannot. The forest people lost.
Their rites hid in the travelling troupe under the seal of *only stories*
[T:184]. Everything since — church, state, market, screen — is a refinement of
the same architecture: a meta-narrative delivered to a seated audience.

**The consummation.** We are in it [T:183]. The axial form is exhausting
itself, not because anyone defeated it but because the medium became aware of
itself [T:76]. Everyone knows it is all constructed. Deadpool looks at the
camera; the church is theatre of morality; the state is theatre of politics;
all the walls come down with a tap [T:185–194]. The hermetic seal around the
meme-plex cannot admit anything new — not even aliens [T:192] — so it cracks.
The fourth wall is furniture. Meta is dead [T:74].

What comes after the consummation of an age is not decided. Two exits are open
and both are bad. **Regression**: rebuild the seal harder — fundamentalism, the
algorithm, the archonic hub that everything flows through [T:30–37].
**Dissolution**: nothing is real, so harvest the openings — the concert, the
festival, the movement as loose harvesting event [T:251]; will in place of
love at the bottom [T:253–259]. The first refuses the knowledge. The second has
nothing behind the frame.

## The third exit

Sixth Wall's wager is a third thing, and it is the whole company.

A civilisation that has discovered its stories are made cannot go back to not
knowing. But it does not have to choose between lying to itself and having
nothing. It can learn to *make stories on purpose — together, in the open, with
love at the bottom and a death built in* — and to let them go when they have
done their work. That is the metamodern move [T:116]: past the postmodern
discovery that the stories are constructed, to the understanding that they
must be, and that what is sacred is the making and the unmaking, not the thing
made.

The stage is where this is rehearsed, and not by accident. The stage is the
one place in axial civilisation where the split was always visibly enacted — a
lit world and a dark house — and where it could therefore always be visibly
undone. The troupe kept the source code of the old participation in costume
for two thousand years. Sixth Wall runs it in public.

So: the rite is not a metaphor for religion. It is religion's source code,
executed where people can watch it execute. Erect the symbol, sincerely. Break
it, so it breaks inside them. Hold them across the place where there is no
story. Return them as people who can now do this themselves. The aim is not a
new religion. The aim is a people who can do what religions did — raise a
symbol together and have it work — without needing to be lied to, and who know
how to let it die.

This is the archaic revival [T:108] one octave up [T:497]. Participation,
recovered, with the knowledge of construction intact. Not the pre-axial
innocence. The post-axial sincerity.

## What non-dual means here

The word recurs because the conversation is conducted from inside it, and it
is easy to flatten. The flattening is to read the company's position as "the
fourth wall is bad, so we break it," or "stories are constructed, so they are
not real." Neither is the position. The position is always both halves.

**Played and player.** The participant "is both being played by those forces,
and is also the director of their own story" [T:112]. Not one or the other.
The gap between stage and house is not an error to be corrected. It is how the
One sees itself — Sophia, being everything, has nothing by which to know
herself, and looks at her reflection, and the one becomes two, and a universe
falls out [T:434–436]. Every production is that look. The rite does not abolish
the gap. It makes it conscious, chosen, and circulating [T:448–450]: a gap with
a current running both ways. The cube does not stop being a cube. It becomes a
cube that knows it is a net.

**Necessary and insufficient.** "You are embracing the fact that you have to
have an external symbol. You're recognising the impossibility of the external
symbol representing the totality" [T:133]. The calf must be worshipped *and*
burned. This is not cynicism (it's only a symbol) and not fundamentalism (the
symbol is it). It is the thing the apparel line is named for: sincerely
ironic. Held with a straight face and a wink at once, and both are true.

**The self is already a performance.** "That was all a character I was
playing. I'm this thing that persists that is maybe pure awareness itself,
wanting to experience itself" [T:239]. So *the play becomes reality* at the
sixth wall is not a trick played on reality. It is a recognition that reality
was never anything else. The distinction between play and world was the axial
distinction. *A world is a play that enough people kept performing* is not a
reduction and not a boast. It is what is left when that distinction is seen
through.

**Ground, held and known as ground.** Behind the frame the players stand on
love, "as something that precedes all of these constructions — or I guess that
even itself is a story. I don't know" [T:253]. Both halves of that sentence are
the position. A bold ontological claim, held firmly enough to call something
down and be challenged by what answers [T:205] — and known, at the same time,
to be a claim. This is not a hedge. It is the only way to hold a ground after
you have seen that grounds are held.

**The world is the one reaching.** "The disenchanted world desires
relationship with us" [T:167]. Re-enchantment is not humans deciding to
decorate a dead cosmos. The cosmos is the one that wants to say hi; it does
not want to be left in the car [T:169]. The first god, bound and made a husk,
is asking. Without a human room, Duncan says, "it feels like I'm doing
productions for the spirits themselves" [T:165] — the other side has been the
audience all along. The sixth wall is two-way glass.

## The walls as cosmogony

Read at this scale, the nine walls are not a production checklist. They are
the anatomy of how a world comes to be and returns — emanation and ascent, the
tree down and the tree up.

I, the spark: the One wanting to see itself. II, the word: the reflection; one
becomes two; *logos*. III, where and when: a world. IV, the front wall: the
veil — the One split into seer and seen. V, the back wall: the seen reaches
back and becomes the seer; agency; the current reverses. VI, the ceiling: the
split dissolves; the story is the world. VII: self and other; love. VIII: self
and no-self; being and non-being. IX: the sacrifice — Brahma dismembered, the
sac punctured — a new universe, or a new idea, and the count begins again one
octave up [T:433–497].

A sixth-walled production recapitulates creation. That is why it can do what
it does to a room, and why the casting principle holds: manifestation runs
downward, and you can only bring someone to a wall from the far side of it
[T:498–504].

## The sacrifice, at scale

Every world-making requires a death [T:496]. The calf burns. The founders have
each died once already — one in an ocean that was himself [T:239], one "who
has already died in a sense" [T:165] — and the initiation that makes a person
fit to hold a wall open is the public owning of the shadow [T:422–427]. You go
through the shame.

At the scale of the age: the death of the transcendent God is not the end of
the sacred. It is the ninth wall of a civilisation — the sacrifice that seeds
the next octave. The conversation has a line for it that a machine pulled out
of an essay and made into a chorus, to its author's irritation [T:177]: *the
poison is the cure; it was always there, buried under empire.* The axial
empire buried participation. The collapse of axial certainty — the poison
everyone is drinking — is the cure, because it returns us to participation,
now conscious.

That is what the company is for. Not to entertain, not to heal, not to build
a brand or a church. To rehearse, in rooms of a hundred people at a time, the
thing a whole civilisation is going to have to learn to do.
