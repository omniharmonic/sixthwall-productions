# The Rite

*The craft. How a frame is built to be broken, from the conversation's
practical passages: the hollow form [T:280–299], the meta-pattern [T:293–297],
the waveform [T:272–276], the unexplained rupture [T:263–271], and aftercare
[T:129–134, T:245–260]. The four movements — erect, break, cross, return — are
a `[PROPOSED]` naming of a sequence that is stated at [T:122–134] without
names.*

---

## The sequence

| Movement | What is done | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Erect** | Raise a structure of meaning, straight. The room finds itself inside the symbol. | [T:122, T:298–299] |
| **Break** | Hold the told meaning beside the hidden meaning. It cannot be both. The frame gives, and gives inside them. | [T:293–294] |
| **Cross** | Past linear narrative into plurality, underworld, void. Beyond language. Transmit. | [T:124, T:259] |
| **Return** | Back as authors: structures of meaning are made, and can be made, and chosen. | [T:126, T:134] |

"Erect a structure of meaning. Destroy it. And then move forward." [T:132]
"Then the ninth wall loops around to the first, perhaps on a different scale."
[T:496] — so: an octave.

## Erect: start with the straightest form

"When you're loosening it up, you need the container to be fairly solid."
"We started with a very straight musical. It had all the things that a musical
has. The long exposition, introducing each character one by one, each one
getting one line at the front of the stage and going to the back." [T:298–303]

"Adding *the musical* to the end of any idea makes it intelligible. Whatever
the first word is that people don't understand, the phrase 'the musical'
afterwards is such a compacted sequence of information around what they're
about to experience." [T:280–281]

"There are tons of frames to inhabit to break. And the most hollow ones are
the most well-known, the most mass-consumed. They're perfect vehicles, because
they've already been hollowed out. They didn't have substance prior; they
were themselves echoes of prior iterations of forms that had more symbolic
meaning than they did. What is Mickey Mouse? He's not a god. He's not the god
of cuteness or play or childlike wonder. He's just a mouse. But he's the most
well-known mouse in the world. Instead of it being gauche to utilise them, it's
the most metamodern thing you could possibly do. Those are the symbols that
everyone's written off." [T:281–282]

"So I think that we should keep doing musicals. And we should keep utterly
destroying what a musical is." [T:289–291]

The forms in use, from the conversation: the musical; the megachurch service
[T:15, T:295]; the variety night [T:272]; the revival tent [Herd bible]; the
game show host, the newscaster, the radio voice [T:263]; the art car as
tabernacle [T:313].

## Break: the meta-pattern

"There is an artifice that's been erected that you've been told has one
meaning. Actually there's a hidden layer of reality underneath the symbol that
means something else, that has not been disclosed to you. And by holding side
by side the thing that you were told the symbol means and what the symbol
secretly means, we break the frame of the symbol itself. It can't be both.

"And then we reveal that even the symbol itself was pointing at something that
actually can't be fully described by the symbol." [T:293–294]

The worked case is the Calf in the megachurch: "By giving it the central
object of veneration in a megachurch, people are asked to meditate on the
effectiveness of the frame. Are you worshipping the frame or are you
worshipping Jesus? By having them reflect on the golden calf and be like —
wow, that feels really good too. That feels like almost 100% of what I feel in
a church." [T:295]

"I wrote off the frame of a megachurch as being for stupid people who can't
see behind the curtain. And yet I'm really enjoying myself now that I'm not
taking it seriously. The thing I thought was true about the golden calf — that
it represents an idol — they're saying it should be worshipped. Because it has
enough bait and switch of the frame and the object, the juxtaposition is
different, and all of a sudden: well, why do I like it? And what does it mean
that I like it? And then you've loosened the bolts on their ideological
plumbing enough for things to start jiggling. And then you drive that
beyond-language delivery where they experience it. And because they've been
loosened up, then it shatters." [T:296–297]

## Cross: the rupture that is not explained

"A woman painting a green spiral on a rolled scroll on the floor, speaking
almost Joycean gibberish that is not gibberish — it's a series of shibboleths
or passwords. Then breaking into song when a drummer plays. And there is no
explanation for it. It transitions into the next scene. And then a character
able to immediately shift scope to a faux-familiar thing — a TV newscaster, a
game show host, cheerful demeanour but clearly unblinking eyes — without
acknowledging what just happened at all.

"Allowing that thing that just happened to be forever unexplained, and leave
people's brains racing to put it in a context that they understand. It is
good because it is art, but it is unexplained, and it is unable to be defined
exactly as art. It had spiritual power behind it. It can't be slotted into a
frame, and it is not attempted to be made sense of. To us these elements
actually represent psychic transformation, or a kind of rupture in the
collective, or an unspoken voice. Somebody coming into the context of it would
psychically feel that there is a meaning to it, that there is a context to it,
that it is not going to be explained to them, and that other people are
holding that in the container of their awareness. It does mean something. And
we are not exposing ourselves to the tyranny of that needing to mean
something.

"That's the feminine element: not over-explaining the rupture, and having the
rupture be artistic enough and well-contained enough that it is not disruptive
while disrupting. The art that can be named is not the art." [T:263–269]

Two of Duncan's own ruptures are in the record, and belong in the company's
memory: the polluted-river creature who vomited fifty yards of knotted
rainbow silk for five minutes while a woman sang dolphin-tone opera and thanked
it on behalf of the community [T:269]; and the snake-charmer's microphone,
treated as an adversarial puppet and sheathed without a word [T:271]. The first
was seen a week before it was performed. "These living symbols tell me that
they need to be expressed at a certain point, without explanation." [T:271]

On why it works: "People love to do psychedelics because they will be met with
something that is not of their control and that they do not have context
for." "When the mind knows the context of the consumption beforehand, it gets
compared to all other possible similar variants of that thing." [T:276–277]

## The waveform

"Everybody was there and they knew approximately what hour they'd go on, but
it was all vibes-based when the next performer would go. Very curated variety
show, and the curation was based on the collective energy. And there were no
breaks. When such a thing is held for more than an hour and forty minutes,
people start going into a collective trance state when there's not an *and now
for a pause, weren't these acts great, wasn't this an established form and
frame that you can understand.* The one thing informed the next thing. It can
still be some anchor performers and a bunch of people bringing their gifts —
stone soup — but it has to be so threaded together that the entire thing is a
waveform." [T:272–273]

"The transitions between — that's why I was so furious about the afterparty.
The transition between the ritual and it was almost deliberately botched, to
off-gas all of the energy into the void, and there to be this pit of confusion.
These wall breaks and transitions between established forms are so important
to have seamlessly coordinated. Because when somebody is thrust into confusion
there will be two things that are not merging in their mind, because there are
two things that have never merged before." [T:273–274]

"Having someone with a singing bowl come into the back of a concert and
spread flowers over the audience and then devour the rest of the flower on
stage. Somebody coming through and bringing a metaphor that commands the
attention of people without that thing having been announced and prepared
for." [T:276]

**Operating rule.** No intermissions. The running order is a waveform, not a
list. The transitions are rehearsed harder than the acts. An afterparty is
part of the rite or it is a leak.

## One organism; the many, not the one

"The entire group had been so successfully attuned by doing the established
form meditations beforehand, and the magic of the place was there, that they
were coordinated to be one organism." [T:145] "I love most when a group of
people is coordinated to go off the rails together, and plays in that place,
and the collective energy is actually collectively felt and summoned, and
collective sense-making is made necessary to get through the forest."
[T:151]

"Energies — dare I say entities, or archetypes — are experienced only when
exceptional individuals who have been initiated into those archetypes call
them down, and through a group's focusing on the channel for that person to
pull them through. Movie stars have this quality; ayahuasca servers; really
credentialed indigenous medicine people. One-to-the-many. That seems to be
required to open the portal to these realities — except in cases where a group
of people has the coordination to actually get there together, led by,
ideally, multiple people, so it's not just me as Trickster Shaman keeping the
portal open, because then it's the many-to-the-one again. I can do that. I
can get better at stand-up and do that. That's the established form for that
in white culture — the one-to-many trickster giving people the banishing of
cathartic laughter.

"But the many going through the forest together — the moment of collective
excitement and uncertainty of a whole group of people who are not sure what
is going to happen next, but feel sufficiently held by the quality of the
performance to unify and grow agentic in the moment — that opens a unified
path to some of these collective archetypal forces that generally get
concentrated in a few special individuals. That is the path I'm most
interested in cultivating. Doing it as an individual feels selfish, almost
like stealing. But also — I don't want to get there if others aren't getting
there." [T:155–165]

"I think that the disenchanted world desires relationship with us." [T:167]

## Aftercare and containment

The theology is in `02`; the rule is here.

- A broken frame is an open door; a door does not check who comes through.
  [T:251]
- Most mass ecstatic events are harvesting events. [T:251] Ours must not be.
- Psychosis is a person broken open and left there. [T:126, T:251]
- Front-end screen: neuroplastic, not rigid. Back-end screen: stable enough to
  stand in the void and reconstruct. [T:133–134] Know who is in the room.
- The holding, the transitions, the return, and the afterparty are the
  containment. They are rehearsed.
- Behind the frame, the players stand on love. [T:253] A player who does not
  know what they stand on should not be given a wall to open.

"Monasteries have really good aftercare. They don't let you go through these
gates unprepared, because they know that they can destroy you." [T:130]

## The critique to answer

From the Star House cauldron, a serious viewer's note: "There were twelve
different traditions quoted, and I don't see the thing that unites them all."
[T:143] Duncan accepts it: the same critique applies to leftist multicultural
spirituality in general [T:145]. The answer is not another tradition. It is the
rite itself — that the thing uniting the forms is the making and breaking of
forms, held in love, returned as agency. If a production cannot say that in
one sentence it has not earned its plurality.
