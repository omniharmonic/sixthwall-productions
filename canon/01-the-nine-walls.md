# The Nine Walls

*Duncan's articulation, given in full at [T:433–504], with Benjamin's
interpolations, the geometry, the correspondences he named, and the casting
rule that follows. This is the spine of everything else.*

---

## Two geometries

The walls can be counted two ways, and both are in use.

**The net.** The site renders the six walls as the six faces of a cube,
unfolded into a cross: the crossing is I, then II north, III south, IV west,
V east, VI the foot. This is the visual contract of the home page and it is a
poetic correspondence — six walls, six faces — not Duncan's geometry.

**The dimensional ascent.** Duncan's own account [T:440, T:458] does not make
the first two walls faces at all:

| Wall | Dimension | Surface of the stage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | a point | — |
| II | a line | — |
| III | space and time | the floor and the two side walls |
| IV | — | the front wall: the proscenium, facing the house |
| V | — | the back wall: the house coming in |
| VI | — | the ceiling: the cauldron spilling over into reality |

"The first two would be a point and a line. Theoretically the third would
incorporate the entire cube, but it's just the floor of the stage and the two
walls it happens in. The three walls being foundation, space, and time. The
front row of the proscenium, the audience; the back row, their participation
in the verb; and the ceiling, the whole cauldron bubbling out and spilling to
alter people's embodied experience of participating in reality." [T:458]

The ascent is the truer geometry and the pages use it. The net is the picture.

## I · The Spark

"The seminal spark of the first animating idea. It is the almost libidinal
urge for creation, or the vision that spontaneously emerges in the mind. It is
the animating desire to create, that will to come into being — the sincere
hunger or yearning or consummation of relationship, of connection, the first
arc of the story, the first idea, the thing that needs to be done, the thing
that wants to be done. Without that initial desire there is no authentic
gnosis. There is no authentic emanation; any other emanation would be playing
on other people's borrowed sparks, or things that have been done before."
[T:433]

Benjamin's gloss, Sophia: "By being the totality there is nothing outside of
oneself that could be interacted with as a means of knowing oneself in
relationship. By being everything, you lack the ability to experience yourself,
and so there's this infinite longing to be able to experience oneself that is
what causes her to look at her own reflection. And by seeing the reflection,
the one becomes two, and that's what births an entire universe." [T:434–436]

Correspondence: **the Emperor** — and "the shakti of creation, which is also
held in the first one." [T:439]

## II · The Word

"The first spark, the first urge, that first reflection then expresses itself
in words. When the actual *dialogos* with the initial impulse comes through,
that becomes the written word." [T:437–439]

Correspondence: **the High Priestess** — "an encapsulated magical text, which
is the actual blueprint." [T:439] "The text is what you are saying, what you
are invoking with it." [T:440]

`[PROPOSED]` The site's phrase for this — *an invocation that has agreed to
hold still* — is mine. It is faithful to "what you are invoking with it" and
to the High Priestess as sealed book, but it is not a phrase either founder
used.

## III · Where and When

"The third dimension is the actual three-dimensional space. It is the where
and when. Once you have a place and a time that you are setting this text in,
you've done the integrity of booking theatre. You've done the integrity of
rehearsing the words into people who are acting them out. You have the
production design, the coordination, all of that, to get those words well-lit
and well-presented. That is the third wall." [T:440–441]

Foundation, space, time. The pages add "the drawing of the circle" — that is
from the original site copy, retained; it is consistent with the magic-circle
framing in Duncan's theological statement.

## IV · The Front Wall

"The fourth wall is the one facing the audience. It is the wall break of
communicating with other people." [T:440] "The curation of the audience. That
also includes the marketing, the process of getting them there, at that space
and time. Who is this for? Who do we want to come? How much are they charged?
Is it patrons? What is that navigation of the relationship? And then
communicating with them directly, not just with the text, but with an embodied
experience of energy — actual techniques to open their energy and
cross-pollinate with their energy, which differs ever so slightly from a
traditional theatrical production where actors are drawing the audience into
their world. The active circulation of stage theatre can be so much more
actively shamanic when more performers are deliberately doing energy work on
the audience as part of their practice." [T:441–444]

"It's not just 'isn't it cool, we're in a Shakespeare play' — or Deadpool, or
whatever passes these days. It's the whole process of the audience coming to
this place and time and being willing to donate their attention, or their
attention plus an amount of energy, to receive a circulation." [T:446–450]

**The metaphysics sits here.** "The separation between the audience and the
performers is the same separation between the spirit world and this world."
[T:106] The pages put this claim at Wall IV because it is the wall that *is*
the gap. That placement is editorial; the claim is verbatim.

## V · The Back Wall

"The fifth wall is actually empowering the audience to become more agentic
within their own lives. Instead of merely watching the verb, certain members
of the audience can be bold enough to participate in the verb — or a version
of it is extended to everybody, in a way that doesn't shame them if they're
not willing to cross that line. This is where things like authentic relating
exercises, or collective toning, or an ecstatic dance in the middle of it, or
speaking your poetry into a microphone for the first time, with others, held
in exquisite frame that'll make even the least sophisticated person feel and
sound beautiful. And having that drift at the right time where it will not
dispel the collective experience that everybody is entranced in. This would
be the back wall. The front wall is the actors to the audience; the back wall
is the audience comes in." [T:450–452]

"Getting people to realise that their impulses have anima. They have fire.
They have magic. They have mana. They have truth. And that it is possible to
bring that into life — that incredibly vulnerable moment of creation. Through
getting into these places and vibrating with people rather than simply
consuming the vibration of others and being inspired passively." [T:453–454]

## VI · The Ceiling

"The sixth wall is when the play becomes reality. When some cultural artifact
of the thing actually shifts the way people are interacting with reality, and
if it's very successful, actually shifts the experiences of other people who
haven't even seen the thing. They weren't even there, but the practices, the
techniques, the vibrations that were demonstrated there have shifted enough
real lives that they have inspired changing the way that a business is run.
Or they've inspired other people to create their own performances. Or they've
inspired more collective works of art to be created as part of a wider
culture. Or they've shifted people's nature of the good, the true, and the
beautiful, to start street preaching again, proselytising, being generous,
giving out baked goods — milk and cookies for everybody. There's a crossing of
the hearth that now happens in the structure of reality, and in the extreme
case, the world depicted in the production is the one that is locally held as
truth by some people. That would be the ceiling." [T:456–457]

The site's line — *a world is a play that enough people kept performing* —
predates the conversation and is confirmed by it exactly.

## Past the ceiling

Duncan: "The seventh, eighth and ninth wall are more —" and then, with a
caveat the canon keeps: "I was really stretching for what that seventh, eighth
and ninth would be, and how it was grafted onto established Gnostic and
mystical structures, and that was the best that I could come up with."
[T:482, T:497] He also says: "I have a document that has articulated the ninth
wall and how it loops in and how it's distinct from being and non-being."
[T:497] **That document should be found and this section revised against it.**

**VII · Self and Other.** "If the sixth wall obliterates the distance between
theatrical production and culture — or between cult and culture — the ethical
cult has evolved to be a part of culture; the weird cult is now an established
religion; Mickey Mouse has gone from a gleam in Disney's eye to a theme park;
it exists independently of its creator in the cultural lexicon now. The
seventh wall is bridging the gap between self and other. Seeing and feeling
the self in others; seeing and feeling these verbs that comprise self
beautifully expressed in and through other people. Loving your neighbour as
yourself is the break of the seventh wall. The unitive function of psychedelic
love is that wall break, where the self is actually felt in the other, and
enough encrusted self-story is transcended to share that co-being. These
collective stories get in the way of sharing self stories." [T:486–492]

**VIII · Being and Non-being.** "The obliteration of the barrier between self
and non-self, or between being and non-being. That is the highest form of
Tibetan Buddhism that I have the decency to even know about. The amount of
discipline that it requires to actually break that wall — and the seventh, for
that matter; the amount of actual trauma processing and compassion that it
takes to break the seventh wall — is why I haven't." [T:492–494]

**IX · The Sacrifice.** "The sacrifice of Brahma to make the world. If the
eighth is between being and non-being, the ninth is the explosion, the
transcendence of being and non-being, that sparks the germination of a new
universe — or, in the micro, a new idea. So the ninth wall loops around to the
first wall again, and the whole process continues, perhaps on a different
scale. The child is birthed into the womb; it stretches the amniotic sac, and
then it punctures that and is born into the new reality." [T:496]

## The casting principle

"In order to initiate somebody, or a group of people, into a certain wall —
to get a bunch of people to break the fifth wall — you need to have enough
people capable of breaking the sixth wall. You need to have broken the sixth
wall for yourself. The play needs to be real for you. Within that play, to
inspire the audience to have enough trust to come onto a place that is as
destabilised and uncertain as the stage.

"Further still, I believe that I need to love other people as I love myself
in order to inspire them to break the sixth wall — to create culture in their
own world, or for the world of the play to re-enchant the world, to make
reality a play. I have to see and feel and desire that possibility of
enchantment, and actually feel it, to inspire them to join that reality
rather than the safe and established reality of participating in a world that
they have inherited but not chosen.

"In order to get people to break the fourth wall, you have to have broken the
fifth wall. You have to have transitioned from an audience member to a stage
member to talk to an audience from the stage. By definition. So in filling
these roles: to get people to break the fourth wall, you have to have broken
the fifth. To get them to break the fifth wall, you have to have broken the
sixth. There'll be actors, and they read the lines that have been written for
them, and they do these awesome things, and they feel the emotions, and they
interact as the script details. They have broken the fifth wall. They have
not broken the sixth wall. You need a few people who have broken the sixth
wall to perpetuate the energetic magic and illusion of it." [T:497–504]

Stated as a rule: **to open wall N for others, you must have passed through
wall N+1 yourself.**

A corollary Duncan draws about casting pools [T:510–518]: one city is rich in
people who have broken V but not VI — performers of other people's words; the
other is rich in people on a VI trip who are afraid of V. A production needs
both and usually has to supply the missing one.

## On Dalí

"Salvador Dalí knew what he was talking about when he did the nuclear Christ
on top of the chessboard. The cube of Saturn unfolding into a cross." [T:459]
"Mastery of craft allowed him to evolve to mastery over form itself. Were he
not the artist he was, he would not earn the right to such sorcery. His
technique remained impeccable through the entire arc of his career. The
capacity to naturalistically depict these dream worlds did cross the sixth
wall. The world became Dalí-pilled and still is. He melted the clocks. He
crossed the cube." [T:474–482]

This is the doctrine of the home page's finale, where the unfolded cube
becomes the eight cubes of *Corpus Hypercubus*.

`[PROPOSED]` **The hypercube as the nine walls.** A cube's net is six squares;
a hypercube's net is eight cubes; the Dalí cross is the second. If the six
walls are the cube — the production — then the eight cells of the unfolded
hypercube can be read as walls I through VIII, and the ninth wall as the
*folding*: the motion that closes the net back into a four-dimensional solid,
which cannot be depicted from inside three dimensions and which returns the
figure to a single cell at a higher octave. The ninth wall "loops around to
the first" [T:496]; a net that folds returns to its first cell. This is a
correspondence the conversation reaches toward — it discusses both the nine
walls and the Dalí cross within a few lines — but does not draw. Duncan
should decide whether it is true, useful, or merely pretty. If it is accepted,
the home page already depicts it.
