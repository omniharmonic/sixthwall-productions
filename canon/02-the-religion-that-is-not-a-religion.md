# The Religion That Is Not a Religion

*The metaphysics. Benjamin's articulation at [T:95–138], with Duncan's
additions on the axial turn [T:183–209], and Benjamin's on non-duality, the
doorway, and what is behind the frame [T:236–260].*

---

## The denaturing

"The denaturing of ritual into theatre is the same larger process as the
denaturing of spirituality into religion. In the initial experience of it
there are no spectators. There are only participants. And there's actually no
boundary between this world and the spirit world. I have the embodied
experience of my direct participation as an embodied member of this
cosmological process, that I experience through archetypal narratives working
themselves out, and I recognise myself as embedded within that. And the
ritual keeps the rhythm of our collective coherence of that story." [T:96–98]

"Which is, in some ways, an evolutionary social function to keep us connected,
to do things together, because we're a tribal species. And on another level
it's us acting out real meta-narratives that are actually occurring at the
spiritual level." [T:100]

"The denaturing has us now as passive consumers of ideologically conditioned
experiences. We're still being fed a meta-narrative, but it's not one that we
participate in. It's one that we purely consume. And so the separation between
the audience and the performers is the same separation between the spirit
world and this world." [T:104–106]

## The archaic revival

"The re-enchantment through ritual theatre is that archaic revival that
Terence McKenna talked about, where by breaking those additional walls we're
helping people remember that they are themselves direct participants within
the story of reality, and that they're not passive consumers. That they are
both being played by those forces and they're also the director of their own
story, and that they get to choose how they want to engage in the larger
processes of reality." [T:108–112]

"Breaking the walls of theatre — restoring ritual theatre as a kind of
essential rite of a coherent body of people — is no different from the
religion that's not a religion." [T:114]

**This is the identity the whole site rests on.** The rite and the religion
are not two offerings or two pages. They are one thing described from the
stage and from the altar.

## The loop of the metamodern mind

"The purpose of that religion is to recognise the constructed nature of story,
but then to pass through the postmodern into the metamodern, to then realise
that stories *must* be constructed — and therefore the main thing that matters
is the process by which those stories are intentionally created and dissolved,
to be in an immanent, non-dual, direct phenomenological experience of the God
that cannot be named, because it is —" "All experience." "All experience."
"Including what you have yet to uncover. Including that which you can never
experience by being in a human form." [T:116–121]

"So: erecting the symbol, using that theatrical context — they see themselves
in the symbol that's been erected. And then in the breaking apart of the
symbol, it breaks apart inside of their own internal experience, and then they
have to themselves cross the threshold, much like an initiation, into a space
beyond linear meta-narratives, into the actual underworld, where there is a
kind of plurality which is, in some ways, a type of void. From which they can
then directly participate in reality in a reconstructive context." [T:122–126]

"Where they could go insane. That's the real risk." "That's where theatrical
care is very helpful." "And that's why monasteries have really good aftercare.
They don't let you go through these gates unprepared, because they know that
they can destroy you." [T:126–130]

"The cultivation of that capacity to erect a structure of meaning, destroy
it, and then move forward with something genuinely new that's made possible
by actually touching the immanent experience of God — as opposed to the
abstract story that you stay stuck in a mental loop around — by intentionally
creating them and breaking them. You are embracing the fact that you have to
have an external symbol. You're recognising the impossibility of the external
symbol representing the totality. And then you're bringing people through
that loop, which is itself the developmental loop of the metamodern mind,
which includes the developmental capacities to then erect shared structures
of meaning together.

"But you have to have a certain level of neuroplasticity on the front end,
because rigid, dogmatic people cannot engage in this type of creative play.
And then once you've broken them open, they have to have the psychological
stability to be comfortable existing in that non-dual state, where things
don't make sense and there isn't a coherent overarching meta-narrative — and
then to be the agent of their own self-reconstruction. That, to me, is the
religion that's not a religion." [T:132–136]

## Why not a religion

"Because it's not focused on the external object. That's where they all decay.
Even Buddha gave the clearest instructions to not worship him, to not make an
idol out of him — if you see me in the street, kill me. Anything that claims
to be *it* is probably not it. So it's better to err on the side of: don't
ever put your faith in that external thing. It's always redirecting it back
into the relationship between self and no-self — and self and other, which is
where it goes from here. The Buddha being the Sangha. I don't know how you put
that on a business card." [T:138]

The answer to the business-card problem is the architecture of the site: the
card is the home page, and this is what is behind it.

On the word itself: "What is that *religio* — the re-yoking to principles?"
[T:175] Duncan asks it of himself, about his own resistance to structured
ritual and his knowledge that structure is needed to open the collective void
space. The name is kept, negated, because the etymology is exactly right.

## The axial turn, and where the pagans hid

"From an evolutionary fitness perspective, axial religions will always
outcompete pluralistic religions, because a unified, transcendent notion of
God is a much better way to get large groups of people to kill other people
than the psychotechnologies of how to gradually regenerate a forest over
thousands of years. All the people who knew how to regenerate a forest got
slaughtered by all the people who could unite under one very strict, top-down,
mandated order of morality.

"And that would then drive these pagan rituals into these travelling troupes
that would use the artifice of the stage to conceal and hide the metaphysics —
in a context that, because of the way it presents itself as being outside:
*these are just stories being acted out, they're not powerful meta-narratives
that help people determine what the good is, they're trivial things to
entertain people.* That is itself the ironic distance that now needs to be
brought back to religion — recognising that it also was a large piece of
performance art to get everyone into a shared psychic field to be able to do
things." [T:183–184]

"It is very clearly theatre." "Theatre of morality. Theatre of politics,
theatre of religion. All of the walls are coming down." "They've all broken
the fourth wall. The fifth wall itself is broken." [T:185–191]

"But the walls can't hold, and all they take is a little tap, and yet the pure
takedown of them is not even entertaining." "It's not that it's not
entertaining. To take them on in their own ground is to invite an off-gassing
of violence that is catastrophic and ineffectual. Hence the necessity of
modulating stories. You take it on in the story world." [T:194–197]

And the condition for doing so: "Having an ontological claim about the nature
of reality is key for a shaman to successfully utilise the form to call down
forces. That unflinching gravity of conviction and transmission. The
established form is going to see any claim to meaning-making within it as the
working of a trickster. The necessity is a bold ontological claim that is also
sufficiently true for those willing to claim it to risk being taunted or
challenged by the counterforces that are called down for holding that firmly —
even if it's leavened with enough comedy and uncertainty." [T:205–206]

## The fourth wall is furniture

"When Fox Cinema is including fourth-wall breaks, it means the medium is
sufficiently aware of itself that that's not going to have an effect on an
audience any more. They're aware that the media is aware that it is media."
[T:74–76] "That is an innovation of cell phones, and the scrolling behaviour,
and meme culture, and targeted advertisements — direct communication of media
to the person. That has primed people to normalise those fourth-wall breaks.
The fourth-wall break is now just the third wall. It's an established part of
the performance, expected by the consumers — winking at the fact that they are
consumers in order to normalise that form. The normalisation of a fourth-wall
break to dispel the anxiety of the consumers for their machines trying to get
them to buy products all day is an establishment of the division between
consumer and producer, rather than bringing it closer." "The consummation of
that distinction." [T:77–94]

## Non-duality, the frame, and the two terrors

"There's a kind of cognitive safety that emerges from having a frame that
holds the illusion of consistency — that you know who you are, what the world
is, what your relationship to the world is, and how you should behave and
function in order to participate in it. Discovering that's semi-arbitrary and
constructed is really disorienting for people. It reconstitutes the meaning
they're making of every other moment prior to that, where they've lived as
though that story was the only story, and all of a sudden they feel a
tremendous amount of shame or guilt or fear or anger that they were duped.
And on the other side, it's also terrifying to not have a frame.

"What makes shamanism so interesting to me is that there's a way of swimming
inside of non-duality that retains some — when reality dissolves and the only
thing left is this shimmering awareness, or an ocean of being. Those are the
two characteristics I experience of the masculine and feminine aspects of the
deepest layer. That at the deepest level is really nothing. Underneath is a
mystery. Why that and not anything else? And that unknowability is humbling
and overwhelming for most people. And then you have to come back." [T:236–239]

"The guardrails are much less strict than people actually believe that they
are." [T:243] — with the one exception named: the story that has teeth, the
collective agreement that some people may put you in a box you cannot leave.
[T:239–241]

## The doorway, and what is behind the frame

"The boundary between being and non-being is actually very thin. And most
stuff that doesn't exist wants to exist. To have your hand on the joystick is
something that many forces, entities, wavelengths of the spectrum of
consciousness all want to have. The more agency you have, the more forces want
to be a part of that. Which is why most of these things are loose harvesting
events — they're focalising the attention and they're opening people up.
There are all sorts of entities feeding off of the energy being directed
there. And in the same way, when you shatter the frame, the reason psychosis
comes out of that process is that you've opened the doorway, and anything can
come in and start driving around. 'Oh good, they don't believe in capitalism
any more; I'm going to have them believe in an ancient cow deity.'

"The shamanic capacity is to work with those entities at the level of their
own reality. To be able to say: you can hang out for a little bit, but no — I'm
clear that behind the mask, behind the frame, I'm connected to love as
something that precedes all of these constructions. I've also been to other
parts of the spectrum and it's like: no, everything is will. Blind will.
That's one of the other main ones. So many of these dark sorcerers have also
broken the frame, but the thing they're connected to on the other side of the
frame is will as opposed to love. When love and will and power come together,
I think that is maybe even more whole.

"We have to break the frame. We have to cross into the threshold where we are
beyond language, into phenomenological experience, to actually transmit
something that's more true than the artifice can be. And the shamanic
characteristic of that is the ability to navigate non-duality so that when the
gates are opened there's still some degree of containment to responsibly hold
people across the threshold of their own transformation — without exposing
them to entities that would love a chance to have some fresh meat to drive
around." [T:245–260]

The site says *love* without the entity language, because the site is the
card. This text keeps the entity language because it is what was meant, and
because the aftercare doctrine in `03-the-rite.md` makes no sense without it.

## Read non-dually

Everything above is misread if it is read as a set of positions held against
opposing positions. It is held in pairs, and both halves are true at once:
played by the story and its author [T:112]; the symbol necessary and unable to
hold the whole [T:133]; love as the ground and "that even itself is a story"
[T:253]; the self as pure awareness and as a character it is playing [T:239];
the world as a play and that being no reduction at all. The gap between stage
and house is not a wound to be closed but the way the whole sees itself, made
conscious and run both ways. The full treatment is `07-the-epoch.md`, "What
non-dual means here." Do not write copy from this text without it.
