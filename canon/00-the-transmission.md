# The Transmission

*What Sixth Wall Productions is, said once, for ourselves — and for the spell
we are casting. Asked for at [T:70]: "a mission statement, or a sense of
purpose that we should know for ourselves, but also for the spell that we're
casting." Nine positions, because there are nine walls, and the ninth returns
to the first.*

---

**I. There are no spectators.**
There were never meant to be. In the beginning of every art we practise there
were only participants, and no line between this world and the other one, and
a body of people who knew themselves to be inside the story they were
telling. Everything since has been the slow seating of the participants in the
dark. [T:98, T:104]

**II. The fourth wall is the veil.**
The distance between the stage and the house is the distance between the
living and the spirits, between the laity and the altar, between the consumer
and the thing consumed. It is one distance. Every time we work on the wall we
are working on the veil, and we say so to each other even when we do not say
so to the room. [T:106]

**III. We raise frames in order to break them.**
We do not tear walls down; tearing them down is easy, unkind, and invites
violence. We build them on purpose, in the open, as straight as we can — and
then we break them, with care, so that people see how a world is made and find
that they can make one. The rite is: erect, break, cross, return. Raise the
symbol until the room finds itself inside it. Break the symbol so that it
breaks inside them. Hold them across the threshold where language cannot go.
Bring them back as authors. Then begin again, an octave up. [T:122–134,
T:194–197, T:293–297]

**IV. The straightest form is the best vehicle, and mastery earns the sorcery.**
The musical, the church service, the variety night, the keynote: the more
hollow and well-known the form, the better it carries what we put inside it,
because no one is guarding it any more. But we do not get to break a form we
cannot execute straight. Dalí melted the clocks because he could paint a
clock. [T:280–299, T:474–482]

**V. We build for the sixth wall.**
The one at the exit, through which a play leaves the building and becomes the
way some people live. A world is a play that enough people kept performing.
Money was one. Nation was one. Faith was one. We know how it is done, and we
do it for worlds worth living in. [T:456–458]

**VI. The many, together — not the one for the many.**
A room that has become one organism can reach, all at once, what is normally
reserved for exceptional individuals. The guru, the star, the medicine carrier
hold the many still while the one channels; it works, and it is stealing.
Getting there alone is selfish, and we do not want to get there if the others
are not. [T:155–167]

**VII. Aftercare is half the craft, and behind the frame we stand on love.**
A broken frame is an open door, and an open door does not check who comes
through. The quality of the holding, the seamlessness of the transitions, the
honesty about who can be brought through at all — these are not production
notes; they are the ethics. And when the frame is gone the players must be
standing on something. Ours is love, as a thing that precedes every
construction including this one. Will is also back there, and power. We have
chosen. [T:129–134, T:245–260]

**VIII. We worship no object and we build no brand.**
The idol and the logo are the same mistake: energy trapped in a noun. Every
religion decays at the moment it begins protecting the frame instead of
breaking it; every partnership decays at the moment it begins gathering people
under a name. We cultivate verbs. We hand authorship back. The only object of
devotion is the relationship between the self and what is not the self — which
is to say, the room. If you meet us on the road, kill us. [T:138, T:413,
T:428–429]

**IX. This document is a frame.**
When it has done its work, break it, and begin again.
