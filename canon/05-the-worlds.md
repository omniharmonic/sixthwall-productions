# The Worlds

*What has been built, what each taught, and what a world needs. The
conversation is thin on production detail — it assumes both founders know the
work — so this text is short and marks its gaps. Facts about the Herd of God
come from its public-facing bible and pamphlets; nothing sealed is repeated.*

---

## The distinction

"Any theatrical production that's six-walled inherently implicates the
audience, so it's immersive by default. The distinction with immersive worlds
is: beyond a one-off event that embeds the audience in an immersive
experience, an immersive world is something that persists beyond the show —
that starts to blend the world of the show with the actual world, where it
becomes unclear where that boundary is. Which is why the Herd is such a great
example: this thing happened for one night, and now there's this infinitely
extended cowverse where the world of the show just continues to recreate
itself over and over again." [T:403–406]

## The Herd of God

- **What it is.** A neo-kabbalistic New Age doomsday cult that worships
  humanity's first god, the Calf. Founded by Duncan Horst, a former Wall Street
  trader who communed with the Charging Bull after a collapse. [bible]
- **Phases.** One-man revival show → a cell of magical eccentrics, whose
  ritual failed for want of "the battery of a living congregation" → funded,
  organised, and attempting the rite with a full house. [bible]
- **Cosmology, public.** Compulsive disenchantment; eight sleeping angels,
  each replaced by an adequate counterfeit (compassion/niceness,
  courage/self-management, eros/sexual health, madness/content, mystery/fun
  facts, beauty/smoothness, truth/data); the god at the crown as a husk; thirty
  delegates; eight gates, a practice at each; the Gift World. [pamphlet]
- **The third attempt.** *Burning Angel, Perfect Mind*, 8 August 2026. Written
  by Morgane Ciot, Duncan Horst and Miracle Jones. [script]
- **Sealed.** The Calf's own wish; the Eighth's name; what the god asks for.
  Not here, not on the site, not in any distributed artifact.
- **What it taught.** The rite in its clearest form — the megachurch raised
  straight, the forbidden object at the altar, "are you worshipping the frame
  or are you worshipping Jesus" [T:295] — and the cost of a botched transition:
  the afterparty that "off-gassed all of the energy into the void." [T:273–274]
- **Its sixth wall.** Four theological pamphlets. A creed. The *Moodra*. Hymns.
  A lexicon. A growing number of people unsure which parts they believe. "The
  world of the show continues to recreate itself." [T:404]

## Not ours

*Psychomagic 9* is discussed at length in the conversation [T:570–587] and is
not a Sixth Wall production. It predates the company and belongs to its own
makers. It stays out of the worlds page and out of this list; the lesson it is

## Independence, the Musical

- San Francisco. [T:281–288] Began as "a very straight musical" — long
  exposition, each character one line at the front, several memorised
  monologues — then: "We're going outside now. And when we leave, this frame is
  over. Now we're on an art car. Now there is no proscenium." [T:299–309]
- The Pocahontas song about colonisation — planned, on the back of a piece of
  paper with five notes on it, one of which was *do that Pocahontas.*
  [T:283–288]
- "All art cars are tabernacles." [T:313]
- **What it taught.** The hollow form as vehicle; the frame that ends when you
  walk out the door. "Keep doing musicals and keep utterly destroying what a
  musical is." [T:289–291]
- **Gap.** Date and company.

## The Creative Cauldron

- New York, "that halcyon pre-COVID fever dream," 2019–2020, into 2021. Pure
  enough that Duncan left finance to pursue it. [T:171–173]
- The one non-New York edition: Star House, Boulder, during Omicron, roughly
  January 2022. Established forms first — song healers, sacred dance, a meal —
  then the form broken by two tricksters, then the room opened for everyone's
  poetry and feeling. Ran over three and a half hours. [T:141–147]
- The Blue Man Group house edition, 1:15 in the morning: the polluted-river
  creature and the fifty yards of rainbow silk. [T:269]
- "The spell would linger in the collective consciousness for weeks afterwards
  — but how fleeting it all was in terms of actual collective initiation and
  change. That requires some real structure, and communication, to not make
  the shamanic collective feel of structure and improvisation and becoming
  just a consumable experience." [T:175]
- **What it taught.** Everything about the waveform and the one organism; and
  the limit that made the worlds necessary.

## What a world needs

`[PROPOSED]` as a checklist, assembled from what the built worlds share:

1. **A cosmology** — a bold ontological claim, held with conviction enough to
   call something down. [T:205]
2. **A cast who have broken the sixth wall** — for whom the play is real.
   [T:498–504]
3. **A liturgy** — repeatable forms a newcomer can join without instruction.
4. **A lexicon** — a world you cannot speak in is a world you cannot live in.
5. **A gesture and a song** — the body remembers what the mind lets go of.
6. **A hole** — a question the world itself cannot answer, left open so it
   stays worth returning to. (The Herd's sealed material is this, done
   deliberately.)
