# Lexicon

*The words we use and what we mean by them. Where a term is in-house slang the
entry says so; where it has a source it is cited. Alphabetical.*

---

**aftercare.** The second half of the craft: containment, the holding of the
return, the afterparty as part of the rite. From the monasteries, which "don't
let you go through these gates unprepared." [T:130]

**archaic revival.** McKenna's phrase for the return of participatory,
pre-axial modes of meaning. What the rite is for. [T:108]

**archonic.** Of a certain register of business language — *own the space*,
*be the hub*. The tell. [T:37]

**back wall, the.** Wall V. The house reaching the players; the audience
stepping into the verb. [T:452]

**borrowed sparks.** Vision taken from elsewhere; a world founded on another's
Wall I. Inauthentic emanation. [T:433]

**business card, the.** The problem of saying any of this briefly. "I don't
know how you put that on a business card." [T:138] Solved by the site's
architecture: the card is the home page.

**ceiling, the.** Wall VI, in the dimensional geometry. The surface through
which the play leaves the building. [T:457]

**cow-pilled.** *In-house.* The state of having been brought through a frame
you intended to laugh at. Named for the Herd. [T:200, T:507]

**crossing of the hearth, a.** What happens at the sixth wall: a change in
the domestic, ordinary structure of reality. [T:457]

**cult.** From *cultus*, a thing tended. Not pejorative here. See *ethical
cult*.

**doors, the.** The links at the foot of every page and at the close of the
home scroll. The play world does not stop at the door.

**ethical cult.** A world with a frame that negates itself: it illuminates its
own structure, hands authorship back, and asks no one to sign up their
friends. Half in jest. [T:373, T:414, T:428]

**erect, break, cross, return.** `[PROPOSED]` The four movements of the rite.
[T:122–134]

**front wall, the.** Wall IV. The proscenium; the entire relationship with
the house, including who is asked and what they are asked to give. [T:440–450]

**gift, the.** Not a term of ours — the Herd's *Gift World* — but the horizon
all of this points at: an ensouled world where the passions are the ordinary
weather of a life. [pamphlet]

**harvesting event.** Any mass ecstatic opening that feeds something other
than the people in it. Most concerts. Not ours. [T:251]

**hollow form.** A mass-consumed, fully vacated shape — the musical, the
megachurch service, the mouse — which for that reason is the perfect vehicle.
[T:281–282]

**metamodern.** Past the postmodern discovery that stories are constructed,
to the understanding that they must be, and that what matters is the care of
their construction and the grace of their dissolution. [T:116, T:282]

**Moodra, the.** The sign of the Calf, made with the hands. *AUM* backwards.
[script] Public.

**nouns and verbs.** A partnership establishes a noun; we cultivate verbs.
The idol and the logo are the same mistake. [T:429]

**octave.** What the ninth wall returns to the first at. [T:496]

**off-gas.** What a botched transition does to a room's energy: it leaks into
the void. [T:274]

**one organism.** The state a room reaches after roughly an hour and forty
minutes without a break. Everything that matters happens after it. [T:145,
T:272]

**one-to-many.** The guru, the star, the medicine carrier: the many held still
while the one channels. Works; is stealing. [T:155–163]

**religio.** A re-yoking. Why the name is kept even as it is denied. [T:175]

**rite, the.** The religion that is not a religion, seen from the stage.
[T:114]

**sham man.** What a shaman is when he cannot walk the vulnerable territory.
[T:341–343]

**sincerely ironic.** The company's apparel name, and the metamodern stance
in two words: held with a straight face and a wink at once.

**six-walled.** Of a production: built for the ceiling, implicating the
audience by default. [T:403]

**spark, the.** Wall I. The seminal, libidinal urge to make. [T:433]

**story partnership.** Never brand partnership. [T:413]

**straight form, the.** The container raised without irony, so that it is
solid enough to break. "We started with a very straight musical." [T:298–299]

**the musical.** Add it to the end of anything and people know what they are
about to see. They do not. [T:280–281]

**toroidal.** What comes in goes back out, changed. The shape we want; the
opposite of a funnel. [T:34]

**transition.** Where the energy circulates or spills. Rehearsed harder than
the acts. [T:273–276]

**veil, the.** The fourth wall, seen from the altar. [T:106]

**waveform.** What a production is: one continuous thing, no intermissions,
each element becoming the next. [T:273]

**word, the.** Wall II. An invocation that has agreed to hold still. [T:437]
